The war with Iran is, without question, a miscalculation by the Trump administration.

The purpose of launching the war and the reality of the war have drifted far apart. And with the midterms approaching, the Trump administration is probably getting a little anxious.

---

## The Oil Trade with Russia

In its anxiety, the Trump administration has started buying oil together with the Putin government. There are at least four purposes behind this:

First, profit — through cooperation with Russia, to take a bigger share of the profits in the oil trade. Second, inflation — to ease the damage the war has done to the Trump administration, of which the deadliest part is inflation.

But I believe there is a more important purpose: joining forces with Russia to increase influence and control over the EU.

And the most important point of all: the Trump administration wants to use cooperation with Russia as a side channel to end the Russia-Ukraine war.

---

## The Russia-Ukraine War

The Trump administration's campaign slogan was that it could end the Russia-Ukraine war quickly. Things did not go as wished — the war is still going on. Presumably, nobody believes its credibility anymore — except, of course, MAGA. They are uneducated workers and farmers, easily swayed by public opinion, and they are exactly the Trump administration's electoral base.

Now, with the midterms approaching, the Trump administration is on one hand lobbying Ukraine to give up joining NATO, hoping the war can end quickly; on the other hand, it is trading with the Russian government, using trade to add to Russia's chips for a ceasefire.

To be honest, Russia should also learn to find itself a way to step down. The war has dragged down Russia's economy; if it could sell its energy, Russia could earn a full income and raise its people's living standards. But the war has made its resources unsellable — only countries like China are buying, which is clearly not enough. Not to mention that more than half of Russia's young population has already died because of the war.

In my view, though, both the US and Russia still hold a certain number of chips, and both still have room to negotiate when it comes to Iran and Ukraine.

---

## The Midterms

The latest news is that Trump's wife, Melania, has brokered a localized ceasefire agreement between Russia and Ukraine — in Russian-controlled Oleshky, fighting stops from October 29, so that humanitarian aid can be delivered and civilians evacuated. This is the same line of work as the Ukrainian children repatriation she has been pushing all along.

To be honest: if Trump is not pushing Melania to the front as the heir to his power, then he is using the First Lady's influence to win votes. The current situation is clearly unfavorable.

As things stand, the Republicans' midterms are most likely lost.

The Trump administration's administrative efficiency is indeed not high. Its money-grabbing is plain for all to see, but its policies are unwise. So perhaps we can say: America's democracy is a partial democracy — a democracy for some of the people.

---

## The Stock Market

Now the stock market. War-driven volatility, retail investors fleeing to safety, big money pulling out — the index has seen a large drawdown. It is clear that market liquidity is already insufficient. So even with good news, stocks will struggle to rally quickly — first, because the macro picture is not good; second, because there is not much liquidity left in the market.

Looked at superficially, one might think this was caused by a few large IPOs. But at a deeper level, I believe it is because large amounts of risk-averse money have withdrawn, swapping stocks for cash sitting in bank accounts.

---

## The Iran War

Iran held an advantageous position in the Middle East to begin with. The Trump administration, after being lobbied by certain financial groups, decided to attack Iran — a decision-making blunder from the start. Perhaps it calculated that the war would help with votes. By now, that idea has clearly fallen through.

There might be a better solution: the Trump administration loses the midterms, the Democrats take the lead in negotiating with Iran, and both sides step back and accept certain conditions. That would preserve the Trump administration's face, and let the war end quickly.

So far, the piece of good news is that Russia is trading with the US. The Russia-Ukraine war may well end smoothly in the future — although that is a long-term goal.

And the most important point: oil prices, I believe, will fall sharply very soon.
