There's a component at the heart of every major AI data center buildout that most people have never heard of. It's not the GPU. It's not the memory. It's the connection between them — the optical interconnect that carries data between chips, racks, and systems. And right now, the technology governing those connections is going through a fundamental architectural shift.

That shift is **Co-Packaged Optics**, or CPO. One of the most consequential enabling technologies in the current AI infrastructure cycle. And it's only just moving from prototype to commercial deployment.

---

## The Problem CPO Solves

To see why CPO matters, start with the problem.

Modern AI training clusters need massive, continuous data movement between GPUs, switches, and memory. Models have grown from hundreds of billions to trillions of parameters, and the data flowing between accelerators in a single training run has grown with them. The interconnect handling that flow has become a critical bottleneck.

For decades, the answer was the **pluggable optical transceiver** — a module that slots into a switch or server, converts electrical signals to optical, and sends them over fiber. Fine at moderate data rates. But as bandwidth pushes past 800 gigabits per second toward 1.6 terabits, pluggables hit two hard limits: **power and signal integrity**.

Each pluggable burns roughly 30 watts. A switch with hundreds of ports can need kilowatts just for the optical layer. And the electrical signal has to travel from the switch ASIC across a circuit board to reach the module — at high data rates, that distance destroys signal quality.

CPO fixes this by moving the optical interface right next to the electrical die. Shorter electrical paths. Denser optical fabrics. Lower power. In a CPO design, photonic components sit in the same package as the switching ASIC — no long trace between chip and transceiver, dramatically lower power, better signal integrity at speed.

The numbers: CPO cuts consumption from 15 picojoules per bit to 5, with ambitions below 1. At hyperscale, that isn't marginal efficiency. It's the difference between a power budget the grid can deliver and one it can't.

---

## How CPO Works

A pluggable module is self-contained: lasers, modulators, photodetectors, driver electronics, all in a removable box. The switch ASIC talks to it over a high-speed electrical interface, and the module handles the conversion to optical.

CPO packs the optical components — built on **silicon photonics** — into the same package as the switch ASIC. The silicon photonics die carries waveguides, modulators, and photodetectors fabricated with standard semiconductor processes. An external laser feeds light in; the die modulates it to carry data.

NVIDIA's Quantum-X photonic switch pushes 115.2 terabits per second using two CPO modules. Each module holds a Quantum-X800 ASIC built on TSMC's 4N process — 107 billion transistors — plus six optical components including 18 silicon photonic engines. The 200G-per-lane micro-ring modulators cut power 3.5x.

At GTC 2025, NVIDIA introduced two CPO-based switch platforms: Spectrum X Photonics and Quantum X Photonics. Under the hood is TSMC's System on Integrated Chips (SoIC) technology — the 3D integration infrastructure. The SoIC X variant, TSMC's bumpless hybrid bonding process, stacks logic dies vertically at sub-ten micrometer pitch. Shorter interconnects, less resistance, less latency.

The physics are simple: shorter electrical paths mean less resistance, less capacitance, less signal degradation, less power. CPO isn't speculative. It's applied physics solving a real constraint.

## The Three Demand Vectors

CPO isn't riding one application. Three separate demand sources are pulling it into production at once.

### AI Data Centers

The primary driver today. The US hosts most of the world's frontier training clusters, running NVIDIA H100s and upcoming B200/GB200 systems that need 800G and 1.6T CPO interconnects.

Hyperscaler capex makes this demand structural, not cyclical. Microsoft: $80 billion for FY2026 AI infrastructure. Google: over $75 billion in 2025. Meta: $60–65 billion in 2025 capex, with real money going to next-generation optical networking.

TrendForce estimates CPO shipments will exceed 50 million by 2030, lifting penetration in optical networking from under 1% to over 35%. That's not incremental growth. It's an architectural transition.

### Advanced Chips and HPC

HPC and next-gen chip architectures need the same high-bandwidth, low-latency interconnects. As packages grow — more chiplets per package, more racks per cluster — the electrical interconnect bottleneck becomes universal. Not just AI. Everything.

HPC and AI/ML clusters are expected to grow at 36.96% CAGR over the forecast period — faster than the already-high CPO market growth rate. Large-scale compute is simply more interconnect-hungry than everything else.

### Robotics and Autonomy

The least appreciated demand vector. Possibly the biggest over a long horizon.

Physical AI — robots, autonomous vehicles, drone swarms — does real-time processing under a different constraint than data center AI. Latency isn't a performance metric here. It's a safety constraint. A humanoid or a vehicle making physical decisions can't wait out millisecond delays at the sensor-to-compute interface.

As robotics moves from lab prototypes to factory floors and logistics networks, on-board compute will hit the same electrical interconnect limits that pushed CPO into data centers. The form factor differs — robotics needs miniaturization and ruggedization that data centers don't — but the physics problem is identical.

IDTechEx projects the CPO market past $20 billion by 2036, at 37% CAGR from 2026. Robotics and autonomy are a meaningful slice of that long-term number.

## From Prototype to Production

CPO has been an industry talking point for nearly a decade. What changed in 2025–2026: it moved from prototype to commercial production. That distinction matters for investors.

January 2026: Broadcom confirmed shipping over 50,000 Tomahawk 5-Bailly CPO switches in 2025, and previewed a 200G-per-lane third-generation platform.

November 2025: NVIDIA unveiled Quantum-X800 InfiniBand — 144 ports of 800G CPO, claiming a 63-fold signal-integrity gain over OSFP modules.

2026 should see the first real volume ramp of CPO on InfiniBand and Ethernet switches, with hyperscalers actively trialing.

Trial to volume ramp is the inflection point. It turns a promising technology into a durable revenue stream. The industry is there now.

## Where Value Is Accumulating

The supply chain has distinct layers, each with a different risk-return profile.

### The Platform Players: NVIDIA and Broadcom

**NVIDIA (NVDA)** and **Broadcom (AVGO)** have the most direct CPO exposure at the platform level. NVIDIA via Quantum-X and Spectrum-X; Broadcom via Tomahawk/Bailly.

Broadcom, NVIDIA, and Marvell win most hyperscale sockets by vertically integrating switch silicon and photonics. The advantage is structural: they optimize the whole signal chain from ASIC to fiber instead of depending on third-party modules.

For NVIDIA, CPO is more than a product feature. It's a supply chain strategy. On March 2, 2026, NVIDIA put $4 billion into Coherent and Lumentum — $2 billion each — tied to multi-year purchase commitments and capacity access for scaling co-packaged optics. That's not a hedge. It's a declaration that CPO sits at the center of NVIDIA's next-generation AI infrastructure roadmap.

### The Optical Pure Plays: Coherent and Lumentum

**Coherent (COHR)** and **Lumentum (LITE)** are the two largest pure-play optical component companies with direct CPO exposure. Both supply NVIDIA. Both now carry NVIDIA's multi-year purchase commitments.

Lumentum may hold the strongest hand in lasers. It's currently the only supplier shipping 200G-per-lane EMLs at volume — the critical component for next-gen 1.6T pluggable transceivers. Its CEO calls 2026 "a breakout year for laser chip sales."

Lumentum's revenue rose over 65% year-over-year in its latest quarter, with adjusted operating margin expanding 1,730 basis points.

The case for the pure plays is compelling but concentrated: near-term revenue rides on NVIDIA's deployment pace. High upside when NVIDIA executes. Asymmetric risk when it doesn't.

### The Infrastructure Layer: Marvell, TSMC, Fabrinet

**Marvell (MRVL)** sits in an interesting spot. In December 2025 it agreed to acquire Celestial AI for up to $5.5 billion — a bet that optical interconnects become standard plumbing in every AI data center. Marvell's Teralynx CPO platform offers hyperscalers an alternative to the NVIDIA/Broadcom integrated stacks: a multi-vendor supply chain.

**TSMC** is the enabler most CPO analyses underweight. Broadcom and others have adopted TSMC's COUPE platform — evidence of how central 3D integration and hybrid bonding have become. TSMC's advanced packaging — SoIC, CoWoS, COUPE — is a prerequisite for the heterogeneous integration CPO requires. Without TSMC's packaging roadmap, the CPO roadmaps of NVIDIA, Broadcom, and Marvell don't execute.

**Fabrinet (FN)** is the contract manufacturer behind the laser and transceiver ecosystem. Lower profile. Operationally critical.

## Risks Worth Understanding

The thesis is strong. Three risks deserve honest treatment.

**Technology transition risk.** CPO needs new switch architectures, new cabling, new standards, new data center designs. Large-scale deployments are projected for 2028–2030. The transition takes time, and near-term revenue forecasts vary widely across research firms — real uncertainty about adoption pace.

**Supplier concentration.** Much of the optical market depends on one customer — NVIDIA — iterating architectures at unusual speed. Deep integration into NVIDIA's CPO supply chain pays when NVIDIA executes on schedule and hurts disproportionately when cycles slip or architectural choices change.

**Standards fragmentation.** CPO isn't fully standardized. On-board, socketed, fully integrated — multiple approaches compete, and hyperscalers disagree. Convergence on dominant standards will make winners and losers among today's suppliers.

## The Structural View

Forecasts vary — early market, genuine uncertainty. The global CPO market is expected to grow from roughly $95–120 million in 2025 to over $1 billion by 2034, at 30–36% CAGR. IDTechEx goes further: over $20 billion by 2036 at 37% CAGR. Wide range. The direction isn't in dispute.

Every credible forecast agrees on one thing: CPO penetration in AI data center optical networking goes from negligible to dominant within a decade. The physics argument is settled. The technology is in production. The capital is committed.

What remains — always the harder question — is timing and positioning. Who captures the value as the transition accelerates? Who gets squeezed by the shift from pluggable to co-packaged? Which architectures win the hyperscaler qualification races that decide multi-year revenue streams?

No clean answers today. But CPO as a structural theme — not a trade, a multi-year infrastructure transition with compounding demand from AI, chips, and robotics — is one of the more clearly articulated opportunities in tech right now.
