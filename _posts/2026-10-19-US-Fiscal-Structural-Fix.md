I keep coming back to one point: the key to America's debt problem is pumping the stock market.

The CBO numbers are blunt: in FY2025, federal revenue was $5.2 trillion, of which individual income taxes (including capital gains) were $2.7 trillion — more than half. Stocks up, tax base thick; stocks down, deficit wider. It's that simple.

---

## Where the Revenue Comes From

Individual income taxes, corporate taxes, capital gains taxes — all tied directly to asset prices. Stocks up 20%, and the tax base thickens for years; down 20%, and the fiscal hole widens.

So the stock market isn't just Wall Street's business — it's the Treasury's. A booming market is America's cheapest revenue tool: no tax hikes, no spending cuts, just higher asset prices, and the taxes come on their own.

---

## Stocks, Economy, Revenue

A booming stock market, economic growth, and fiscal revenue feed each other in a loop:

- Stocks rise → wealth effect → consumers spend → growth;
- Growth → corporate profits → stocks rise further;
- Both → more tax revenue → healthier finances → room to keep supporting the market.

Tech stocks spin fastest in this loop.

Capital flows into tech → giants raise money cheaply and expand aggressively → breakthroughs, profit growth → stock prices rise → more inflows. A virtuous flywheel: Wall Street funds it, Silicon Valley grows it, the Treasury taxes it.

Over the past decade-plus, America's best fiscal years were almost always tech's brightest years. Tech stocks are the most efficient gear in the whole stocks-to-revenue transmission chain.

---

## Foreign Capital

The US market has an advantage no other country has: it's the world's capital reservoir.

A rising market draws foreign money into US stocks, with a triple benefit: higher asset prices (thicker tax base), a supported dollar (cheap imports, tame inflation), and stimulated domestic consumption and investment (more taxes). Every dollar of foreign inflow gets multiplied several times over in the fiscal ledger.

That's why America has every incentive to maintain the "US stocks always go up" narrative.

---

## The Structural Part

This is the most important conclusion, in my view: **none of this belongs to any party.**

A Democratic administration would do exactly the same. Obama-era QE pushed up asset prices; Biden-era fiscal stimulus steadied the market — different party rhetoric, identical moves. Because the fiscal math is the same: the tax base is anchored to asset prices, and whoever is president must protect the stock market first, everything else second.

This isn't any administration's invention. It's the structural feature of the dollar–stocks–Treasuries system. The dollar is the world currency, US stocks are the global pricing anchor, Treasuries are the global collateral — bound together, they dictate America's only sustainable fiscal model: "asset prosperity → abundant tax revenue." Any policy trying to bypass this road gets dragged back by reality.

So when analyzing America's fiscal problem, don't start with "what does the Trump administration want" or "what do the Democrats want." Start with "what does the structure demand." The structure always demands the same thing: a booming stock market. Partisan fights are the skin; the balance sheet is the bone.

Once you see this, a lot of seemingly contradictory policies make sense: why markets always get rescued first in a crisis, why both parties are strikingly aligned on "protecting stocks," why every Fed move ultimately points at asset prices. Not a coincidence. Structure.
