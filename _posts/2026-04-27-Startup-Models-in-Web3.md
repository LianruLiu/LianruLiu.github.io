## Introduction: Most Founders Build Products, Few Build Market Structure

Over the past few years, Web3 entrepreneurship has been dominated by narratives:

- SocialFi  
- GameFi  
- AI + Crypto  
- RWA  
- DePIN  
- Meme Economies  

These sectors are not meaningless. Some will create real value. But many projects remain focused on storytelling, branding, or temporary user attention rather than solving structural problems inside digital capital markets.  
If we treat crypto as an unfinished global financial system, then the most durable opportunities are rarely found in consumer hype. They are found in financial infrastructure.  
In my view, the two most valuable startup models in Web3 today are fundamentally simple:  
> **1. Add leverage to low-leverage assets**  
> **2. Add liquidity to illiquid assets**

These two models correspond to the two oldest demands in finance:  
- Increase capital efficiency  
- Reduce transaction friction

Nearly every enduring financial institution in history has monetized one or both.

---

# I. Add Leverage to Low-Leverage Assets

## What Is a Low-Leverage Asset?

Many assets generate stable returns but suffer from low capital efficiency, such as:  
- BTC / ETH spot holdings  
- Staked assets  
- Treasury-backed RWA yield products  
- Blue-chip LP positions  
- Stablecoin carry strategies  
- Low-volatility DeFi income positions

The problem is not that these assets are unproductive.

The problem is:  
> Their return profile is often too slow relative to available capital demand.  
As a result, markets naturally ask:  
- Can BTC be posted as collateral to borrow stablecoins?  
- Can stETH be rehypothecated into new positions?  
- Can tokenized treasury exposure be pledged again?  
- Can low-risk yield assets be layered into higher-yield structures?

This is what it means to add leverage to low-leverage assets.

---

## Why This Model Always Exists  
Every financial system eventually converges toward the same principle:  
> Stable yield assets are repeatedly repackaged through financial engineering.

In traditional finance:  
- Real estate becomes mortgage leverage  
- Government bonds become repo collateral  
- Equities become marginable assets  
- Private funds become NAV lending products

Crypto is following the same path.    
> Convert passive assets into recursively usable capital.

---

## Where the Startup Opportunity Lies

### 1. New Collateralized Lending Systems  
Protocols that support broader collateral classes:  
- Liquid staking tokens  
- LP tokens  
- Tokenized RWAs  
- Yield-bearing claims

### 2. Automated Leveraged Yield Products  
One-click systems that handle:  
- Deposit collateral  
- Borrow against it  
- Reinvest proceeds  
- Auto-rebalance risk

### 3. Risk Pricing Infrastructure  
The true moat is not lending. It is pricing risk.  
This creates opportunities in:  
- Oracle design  
- Volatility engines  
- Liquidation systems  
- Credit scoring models  
- Stress testing layers

# II. Add Liquidity to Illiquid Assets

## What Is an Illiquid Asset?  
Many assets may hold real value but lack active markets, such as:  
- Long-tail tokens  
- Newly launched assets  
- NFTs  
- Community points  
- Locked token allocations  
- Small-scale RWAs  
- In-game assets  
- Prediction market positions  
Their core problem is not valuation.  
It is:  
> Buyers cannot enter efficiently, and sellers cannot exit efficiently.

Without liquidity:  
- Price discovery fails  
- Financing becomes difficult  
- Market size remains capped  
- Network effects never emerge

Adding liquidity therefore creates value directly.

---

## Why Liquidity Matters So Much  
Many investors assume price reflects intrinsic value.  
In reality, especially in the short and medium term, price is strongly shaped by:  
- Whether anyone is trading  
- Bid-ask spread width  
- Market depth  
- Execution speed  
- Slippage under size

A great asset with poor liquidity often trades at a structural discount.  
> Liquidity is not separate from value.  
> Liquidity is part of value.

---

## Where the Startup Opportunity Lies

### 1. New AMM Designs  
Especially for thin markets:  
- Dynamic curves  
- Automated concentrated liquidity management  
- RFQ systems  
- Intent-based execution models
  
### 2. OTC and Secondary Market Infrastructure   
For trading:  
- Private allocations  
- Locked token claims  
- DAO ownership transfers  
- Pre-listing exposure

### 3. NFT Financialization

Including:  
- NFT fractionalization  
- NFT collateral lending  
- NFT market making

### 4. Prediction Market Liquidity Layers

Prediction markets often fail not because forecasting is weak, but because depth is weak.  
> Whoever solves liquidity often wins the market.

---

# III. Why These Two Models Are More Durable Than Narratives

Narrative-driven projects often depend on:  
- Bull market optimism  
- Continuous user excitement  
- Token speculation  
- Short attention cycles

But leverage demand and liquidity demand exist in both bull and bear markets.  
During bear markets:  
- Users need financing  
- Users need exits from illiquid holdings

During bull markets:  
- Users want to amplify gains  
- New assets need market depth

This gives these models structural advantages:  
- Cycle resilience  
- Real cash flow potential  
- Defensible moats  
- Stronger network effects

---

# IV. My View: Future Web3 Giants Will Likely Be Efficiency Platforms

Many assume the next major Web3 company will be:  
- A social platform  
- A game studio  
- A meme application  
- A consumer-facing viral product

Some may succeed.

But the higher-probability winners may be quieter companies doing the following:  
- Increasing leverage capacity  
- Improving liquidity access  
- Reducing liquidation loss  
- Lowering trading friction  
- Accelerating capital velocity

Because in financial markets, hype attracts attention.

Efficiency attracts permanence.

---

# V. Conclusion

If Web3 is becoming a global on-chain capital market, then the most valuable startup opportunities may not be complicated at all.  
They reduce to two categories:  
> Add leverage to sleeping assets.  
> Add liquidity to frozen assets.

The first unlocks capital efficiency.  
The second unlocks market efficiency.

And nearly every great financial institution in history has built around one or both.
