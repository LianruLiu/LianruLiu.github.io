A country's own natural resources and its geographical location determine, in a sense, whether it can become more prosperous in the future.

These God-given, natural endowments limit, in a sense, its strategic options and its level of development.

That is why we say African countries find it hard to develop easily — their geography and climate make development naturally harder for them.

Countries near the sea, by contrast, trade internationally more easily, change their economic circumstances more easily, and promote economic development more easily.

At the micro level, trade benefits local economic development; international trade benefits national economic development.

I think whether it is the US government, the Chinese government, or even European governments, they should all understand this. From the Dutch "sea coachman" of the 17th century, to the British Industrial Revolution, to the rise of the American economy, and even China's reform and opening up and its entry into the WTO — they all relied on international trade.

But Trump pushed a trade war in his first term. I believe he genuinely wanted to change America's economic situation, which is why he pushed the trade war — he wanted to raise tariffs to increase national revenue. But this approach, as we can see now, was clearly a wrong decision.

The Trump administration wanted to use tariff revenue to promote domestic firms, but this development plan is unsustainable and unwise. Without competition, firms can hardly develop sustainably; even without external competition, they will naturally die out over time — because an economy inherently faces cycles of growth and decline.

The Trump administration wanted tariff revenue to raise national income and improve America's debt problem, but the consequences of this whole series of tariffs are equally unsustainable, and tariff revenue is not enough to change the debt problem.

Just like the early part of Milei's term in Argentina, when he wanted to shrink government agencies to cut national spending — and it did achieve certain results, with inflation falling from over 200% to around 30%. But this approach of reducing debt by controlling costs is clearly unsustainable too: Argentina is now facing an economic recession, with the third quarter of 2026 very likely already in a technical recession, consumption falling, and tens of thousands of businesses closing.

A country that wants to control costs, cut government spending, and reduce unnecessary expenditure to change its economic situation may see some results at first, but in the long run, it is unsustainable.

I believe that if a country wants to change its economic problems, the most direct and most critical method is to push for sustained economic development. Democracies need votes; even some non-democratic countries need sustained economic development to stabilize social development.

From a realistic standpoint, if a country has no choice but to face an economic recession, then to maintain its regime it needs to improve technological safeguards and social subsidies, to keep society stable and reduce unrest. I would say that if the economy runs into trouble and heads toward collapse, war is inevitable — and the fundamental cause of a country's collapse is often economic.

We know the economy has cycles: prosperity, stagnation, recession, and then development again. In these unavoidable cycles of recession, a state can raise basic subsidies for its citizens, giving them a certain guarantee, so that when they face unemployment and unstable income, they can still live stably — thereby maintaining the stability of society and even of the regime.

But the economic pressure on Generation Z has already exceeded what those at the top can imagine. Reading *The Pinch: How the Baby Boomers Took Their Children's Future* offers an insight: the upper economic beneficiaries, the beneficiaries of the previous generation, have firmly seized most of society's resources, and so far they have no intention whatsoever of distributing what they have gained to the young people of Generation Z.

So young people all over the world face enormous pressure to survive. Whether it is an East Asian society like Japan, or European societies where young people must survive on their own, or the United States — their young people face unprecedented pressure. And the top policy designers are completely unable to realize that once the pressure on these young workers cannot be released, once they face pressure they cannot change, that is the beginning of a regime's collapse.

And that is only the distribution problem.

Now the world faces the problem of stagnating economic development. So if a state wants to maintain stability, it can raise social subsidies and basic welfare for its citizens, but for that country's economy this is also dangerous — it can only temporarily relieve social pressure, not fundamentally solve the problem.

So if a country wants to change its economic problems, it should really look for a new path of economic development, and through a new cycle of economic development, relieve economic pressure and change the current situation.

From a long-term historical perspective, any problem in human history will eventually be digested by time; humanity will ultimately become a handful of earth, a plate of sand.

But the tragic fate faced by one generation is, in fact, their whole life.

Human life is truly too short.

**Afterword**

Just a few reflections on the recent chaotic market and on countries' economic measures that keep missing the point — for example, the ever more numerous and ever heavier taxes in Europe and America — and a lament for the brevity of human life.

I still hope the US Treasury and the Federal Reserve will take some action to rescue the market — this matters enormously for the world economy.

And I hope this week's meeting between Trump, Putin, and Xi can effectively resolve the war issue.

Human life is so short — why not stop the wars, find someone you love, do what you love together, and go where you want to go (Montreal, Tokyo, for example :)). Young people of Generation Z face so many problems accumulated by the previous generation, and so much of their short and beautiful youth has already been wasted.
