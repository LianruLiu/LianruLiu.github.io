In November 2025, Lighter — a perpetual futures exchange most people had never heard of — processed $292.5 billion in a single month. More than Hyperliquid. More than Aster. More than anyone on-chain.

One month later it launched its token, paid out one of the largest airdrops of the year, and watched most of that volume walk out the door. Today it does around $39 billion a month.

This is not a story about failure. It's about what happens after the incentives end — and Lighter is one of the cleanest case studies crypto has ever produced for how to bootstrap an exchange, and what actually survives contact with reality.

---

## The Timeline

**2022 — The founding.** Vladimir Novakovski, a former Citadel quant, starts Lighter in Miami. Roughly 80% of the early team comes from Lunchclub, stacked with competitive programming talent. The thesis from day one: centralized exchanges are black boxes, and zero-knowledge proofs can end that.

**January 2025 — Private beta.** Invite-only. Season 1 of the points program runs January to September, onboarding 100,000+ users. The clever bit: points aren't awarded for raw volume alone. They weight PnL, Sharpe ratio, and delta-neutral behavior — rewarding skilled trading over click-farm spam. About 250,000 points go out weekly. OTC markets start pricing the points anyway.

**October 2025 — Public mainnet.** October 1–2, no invite codes, no gatekeeping. Season 2 of points launches with explicit anti-Sybil measures. October volume: $272.5 billion. Equity perps arrive (COIN, HOOD), then FX and RWAs via Chainlink integration.

**November 2025 — The peak.** $68 million raised at a $1.5 billion valuation, led by Founders Fund and Ribbit Capital, with Haun Ventures, Dragonfly, and Robinhood Ventures in the mix. A Founders Fund partner says the team is "85–90% of why we made the investment." November volume: **$292.5 billion** — the biggest month any perp DEX had ever printed.

**December 2025 — Expansion and TGE.** Spot trading launches early in the month. On December 30, LIT goes live: fixed 1 billion supply, 25% airdropped with no vesting, 25% reserved for future ecosystem programs, team (26%) and investors (24%) locked for a year then vesting over three. It opens around $2.34–3.00, below the $3.25 pre-market price. The farmers sell.

**2026 — Institutionalization.** Mobile app in January. Python, Go, and TypeScript SDKs; the prover and contracts go open source. Robinhood Wallet integrates Lighter perps for eligible users, with 11 million LIT committed to the Robinhood community. The company formalizes as a US C-Corporation, pledging to run the protocol at cost while progressively decentralizing. In September, Bitwise lists the first Lighter ETP (BLIT) on Deutsche Börse Xetra.

**Today.** Roughly $39 billion in 30-day volume, $487–741 million in TVL, $766 million in open interest, over $1.6 trillion in cumulative volume. A stable top-five perp DEX — on the honest baseline.

## What Actually Worked

Strip out the farming, and five decisions explain Lighter:

**1. A technical wedge, not a feature.** Lighter didn't build "another perp DEX." It built an application-specific ZK rollup where every order match and liquidation is cryptographically provable. The marketing line — "the end of the black-box exchange" — is the product. In a sector where traders are asked to trust the operator, verifiability is differentiation.

**2. Zero fees as a weapon, with a second revenue curve.** Retail pays nothing. The money comes from institutional market makers paying for low-latency API access — payment for order flow, rebuilt on-chain — plus liquidations and treasury income. The zero-fee wedge forced the entire sector to cut fees. Lighter understood that the wedge only works if someone else pays, and named the payer upfront.

**3. Points that reward skill, not spam.** By weighting PnL and Sharpe instead of pure volume, Lighter bought higher-quality liquidity than competitors who paid per dollar traded. It still got farmed — everything does — but the design filtered for traders who might actually stay.

**4. Distribution over brand.** Instead of out-marketing incumbents, Lighter plugged into Robinhood Wallet and let a Bitwise ETP put LIT in European brokerage accounts. Distribution beats advertising when your competitor has a three-year head start.

**5. Compliance posture as strategy.** US C-Corp, run-at-cost pledge, progressive decentralization, transparent on-chain revenue tracking. Whether you believe the decentralization roadmap or not, the posture buys regulatory optionality that offshore competitors don't have.

## The Playbook Worth Stealing

For builders, Lighter's path compresses into something replicable:

- **Pick a verifiable niche.** Don't out-feature the incumbent; make one thing provably true that they can't claim.
- **Subsidize one side, charge the other.** Zero fees for the side you need (retail flow); a clear payer on the other side (institutions buying speed). A subsidy without a named payer is just burning money.
- **Run points before token, and score quality.** Incentivized volume is rented. Score it on behavior you want to keep, and assume 80%+ leaves at TGE — build for the 20%.
- **Borrow distribution.** Wallets, brokers, ETP issuers — anywhere users already are. Don't build traffic from zero against an entrenched leader.
- **Put compliance in the pitch deck early.** The C-Corp structure and at-cost operation aren't idealism; they're how you stay in the game when regulators arrive.

For investors, the checklist is the mirror image: separate farmed volume from retained volume (Lighter's $292.5B → $39B is the template), verify the revenue model exists beyond the whitepaper, and treat team pedigree as the primary asset at the early stage — the VCs literally told us it was 85–90% of the thesis.

## What This Means for On-Chain Investing

Lighter's arc maps onto where on-chain markets are going:

- **Perp DEXs grew up.** From 0.11% of global perp volume in early 2023 to 12.33% today. This is infrastructure now, not an experiment — and infrastructure gets valued on cash flow, not narratives.
- **Everything becomes a perp.** Stocks, FX, pre-IPO names, trading nearly 24/7 outside exchange hours. The line between "crypto exchange" and "everything exchange" is dissolving, and whoever owns the venue owns the flow.
- **The ETP bridge is the new listing.** BLIT on Xetra, HYPE spot ETFs in the US — tokens no longer need a Binance listing to reach traditional capital. Watch ETP filings as a leading indicator of which on-chain assets go mainstream.
- **Buyback-and-burn is now table stakes.** HYPE proved the flywheel; LIT copied the script on day one. The question for every new token is no longer "does it burn?" but "is there real revenue behind the burn?"
- **The honest metric is post-incentive retention.** Every major DEX launch will now be farmed. The only number that matters is the baseline after the airdrop. Lighter gave us the cleanest A/B test in the sector's history: $292.5 billion rented, $39 billion owned.

## My View

Lighter executed one of the best go-to-market plays in DeFi history and then gave us the receipt. The $292.5 billion month was marketing spend, and like all marketing spend, most of it didn't stick. What stuck — $39 billion a month, top-five status, Robinhood distribution, a Bitwise ETP, a real technical moat in ZK-verifiable matching — is a genuinely valuable company.

The replicable insight isn't "do zero fees" or "do a points program." It's the sequencing: **verifiable wedge → subsidized demand → quality-scored incentives → borrowed distribution → institutional wrappers.** Each step converts the previous step's rented attention into something slightly more permanent.

For on-chain investors, Lighter resets the evaluation framework. Don't ask how much volume a DEX does. Ask how much volume it does after the incentives end, who pays for the subsidy, and whether the burn has revenue behind it. Everything else is the first scoreboard — and the first scoreboard is always fake.

---

## Conclusion

Lighter went from a Citadel quant's thesis to $292 billion a month in under four years, then survived the comedown that kills most incentive-driven launches. The playbook — ZK verifiability as the wedge, zero fees as the weapon, skill-scored points as the filter, Robinhood and Bitwise as the distribution — is now the template every perp DEX challenger will copy.

The sector learned something too: on-chain derivatives are no longer the alternative. At 12.33% of global perp volume and climbing, with stocks and FX trading around the clock, they are becoming the market. Lighter didn't just challenge Hyperliquid. It showed what the next generation of that market looks like — and left the receipts for anyone willing to read them honestly.
