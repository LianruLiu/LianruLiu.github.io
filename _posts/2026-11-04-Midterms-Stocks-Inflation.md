The midterm results went exactly as I expected.

On November 3, Democrats took both chambers of Congress: the Senate flipped in Iowa, Kansas, Ohio, North Carolina, South Carolina, and Texas — deep-red states — and the House win was decisive, with Hakeem Jeffries expected to become speaker. Voters delivered a verdict on the Trump administration's second term. This is exactly what I wrote in my October 23 article: the Republicans' midterms were most likely lost.

---

## The Solution I Proposed Is Becoming Reality

In that article I also laid out a "better solution": the Trump administration loses the midterms, the Democrats take the lead in negotiating with Iran, and both sides step back and accept certain conditions — preserving the Trump administration's face, and letting the war end quickly.

Now, the table has changed. Democrats hold Congress; the Trump administration holds executive power. What comes next is the real-world version of that plan.

---

## What Comes Next: Stocks and Inflation

So what comes next? In my view, America's stock market and inflation will become the focus of both parties.

The reason is simple, and it is the same for both sides:

For Democrats, winning Congress is only the beginning. They have to prove to voters that they manage the economy better than Republicans. And the two most visible items on the economic report card are stocks and inflation. Stocks up, and fiscal revenue is secured — the logic I laid out in "The Structural Fix for America's Fiscal Problem": the tax base is anchored to asset prices, and no administration can get around it. Inflation down, and ordinary people's lives get better, and votes hold.

For the Trump administration, having lost Congress, stocks and inflation are its last remaining dignity. Executive power is still in its hands, and market performance and price data are the only credentials it has left to show its base.

So both parties will now compete over the same thing: who can lift the stock market, and who can bring inflation down.

---

## Oil Is the Key Variable

There is one more variable here, which I pointed out before: oil prices.

I said oil prices would fall sharply very soon. If that judgment holds, inflation pressure will ease with it — good news for both parties, and room left for Democrat-led negotiations. Conversely, if oil prices do not fall and inflation cannot be brought down, then whoever is in office will have a hard time.

The midterms only changed the table. The real exam starts now: stocks and inflation.
